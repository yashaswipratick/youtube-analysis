# Coorg Episode Storytelling Playbook
**For:** The upcoming Coorg vlog episodes on Yash Xplore  
**Reference format:** [Yash Xplore Five-Act Travel Story Format](yash-xplore-five-act-story-format.md)  
**Applied learning:** [Bhoga Nandishwara + Avathi Lake self-review](yash-xplore-bhoga-nandishwara-avathi-retrospective.md)

## Why this playbook exists

The Bhoga–Avathi retrospective suggests the main opportunity is not to add more lyrical lines. It is to make the story promise clearer, bring the first meaningful delivery closer to the opening, connect each location to one central idea, and stop repeating information across VO, on-camera moments and the ending.

Coorg has many possible story ingredients—mountain roads, mist/rain, coffee estates, cultural history, waterfalls, viewpoints, resort life, food, a flexible road-trip plan. These are ingredients, not automatically a story. For each episode, choose the one engine that the actual footage can support.

**Keep v2.3 as the AI-ready creative reference in this folder. This playbook is a manual application, not an application prompt change. No saved Coorg episode script is rewritten by this document.**

## 1. The Coorg episode rule: one episode, one promise

Before assembling narration, complete a one-sentence Story Compass for the episode.

- **This episode is really about:** one question, discovery, personal attempt, contrast, or decision—not a list of all places visited.
- **The viewer should stay to learn/see:** a specific result or changed understanding that the footage can deliver.
- **My personal stake:** what I wanted to see, understand, experience, test or decide during this part of the trip.
- **Main evidence:** the two or three strongest real moments/shots/conversations that support the promise.
- **Payoff image/moment:** the strongest real scene that should answer the opening, or change its meaning.
- **Secondary details:** food, directions, hotel features, price and route information. Keep them if they help the trip or story; don't let them displace the main promise.

If the sentence only says “we visited X, Y and Z,” the story engine is not selected yet.

## 2. Choose the engine to fit the captured footage

### Option A — Slow sensory discovery (only if the footage supports it)
**Possible question:** Does Coorg's character appear not just at famous stops but in the little moments between them?

Use actual mist, rain, road through greenery, plantation details, ambient sound or an unplanned stop as evidence. Avoid simply stacking adjectives like “beautiful,” “peaceful,” “green” and “magical.” Each return to mist/coffee/rain should add something new: arrival, obstacle, discovery, a change in light, a decision, or a more specific feeling.

### Option B — Coffee investigation (only if there is a real plantation/guide experience)
**Possible question:** What did I learn by seeing or experiencing how coffee is grown, processed or presented here?

Let the actual guide, process, plantation details or creator questions drive the scene. Use only facts demonstrated by footage or verified with reliable sources. Don't turn one plantation visit into a claim about all Coorg coffee.

### Option C — Place / route discovery (when two or more stops have a clear connection)
**Possible question:** Why was this route or combination of places worth choosing, beyond ticking locations off a map?

Connect the stops through contrast, distance, weather, a shared history, the creator's changing expectation, or one continuous question. Give directions concisely and keep detailed logistics in captions/description.

### Option D — Personal quest / decision (if a real challenge or plan change happened)
**Possible question:** Can I make the planned experience work with the actual time, weather, access and energy I have?

Show only genuine friction and decisions. A road closure, rain, late arrival, tiredness or changed plan can be story material if it actually happened; never manufacture trouble for drama.

**Default rule:** one primary engine and at most one secondary thread. Not every Coorg episode needs all four.

## 3. Five-act plan for each episode

The suggested proportions below are editing hypotheses, not fixed duration rules. Use them to avoid a long preamble and to protect the strongest footage.

### Act 1 — Hook / first delivery
- Open with the strongest real sensory image, event, question or unexpected detail.
- Give the viewer a single reason to care: what is puzzling, being attempted or about to be discovered?
- Put a glimpse of the promised experience early if the captured footage has one.
- Avoid beginning with a long destination description, multiple historical claims, a generic greeting and the full itinerary in sequence.
- **Edit test:** can a new viewer understand the promise and see evidence for it before the setup has become a road-trip diary?

### Act 2 — Setup / moving toward the goal
- Keep the live on-camera moment conversational: where you are, what you are about to do, what decision is real.
- Add only the route/time/weather constraint that changes what can happen.
- Do not repeat the title or re-explain the itinerary after the hook has already introduced it.
- Use safe road footage and natural sound; record all talking-to-camera updates while parked.

### Act 3 — Experience / discovery
For each anchor scene, use the nested loop only where it helps:
1. What did I want to know or try?
2. What did I actually notice, do, ask or hear?
3. What does the footage or real response show?
4. What context is verified and relevant?
5. Why does this detail matter to the episode?
6. Is the question answered or does it lead naturally to the next scene?

Keep a maximum of a few anchor scenes in the main story. Other pretty or practical moments can become short transitions or be saved for another episode.

### Act 4 — Reframe / complication / connection
Pick the real moment that changes the viewer's understanding: a detail you overlooked, a contrast between expectation and reality, a guide's explanation, an unexpected weather change, a plan adjustment, or the relationship between two visited places.

Do not add a philosophical voice-over just because the edit needs an Act 4. If nothing changes the meaning, use a shorter bridge and move toward the payoff.

### Act 5 — Payoff / close
- Return to the opening question or image.
- Give a specific answer or a precise personal reflection grounded in the day.
- Ask one relevant audience question, if useful.
- Add a concise like/subscribe CTA if desired.
- End once. Avoid a complete recap, two versions of the same comment question, a long CTA, and then another goodbye.

## 4. The VO / on-camera split for Coorg

Use this split to protect the natural travel-vlog presence:

| Use on-camera for… | Use voice-over for… |
|---|---|
| Arrival and actual first reaction | Context that helps interpret an observed detail |
| Questions, choices, unexpected things and real activity | Connecting two places/acts or returning to an established motif |
| Actual cost/route/availability notes when useful in the moment | Verified history/culture/coffee context tied to an image |
| A genuine plan change or food reaction | Reflection after the creator has had the experience |
| Brief wrap and audience connection | Transitions that would otherwise need multiple itinerary sentences |

**Anti-duplication check:** If you already said “we're now going to X” on camera, VO should not repeat the destination and the route; it should add a reason to care about the next scene. If the visual shows mist, don't use VO only to say “there is mist.” Add a new detail, question, contrast or meaning—or leave the sound alone.

Keep recorded OC speech verbatim. Scripts should guide a shoot, not make spontaneous footage sound memorised.

## 5. Apply the Bhoga–Avathi lessons directly

| Lesson from the self-review | Coorg application |
|---|---|
| Arrival came around 2:09 in a 7:03 video | Use the strongest real moment early; compress nonessential drive/logistics before the first story delivery |
| History details were introduced as a list | At a temple, coffee estate or cultural site, choose one anchor question and use a visible detail or real expert/local explanation as evidence |
| Temple and lake had a strong stone/water contrast but it appeared late | If Coorg scenes have a genuine recurring motif, introduce it early and develop it; do not force “mist/coffee/nature” into scenes that don't support it |
| Directions and timings took a long part of the transition | Keep spoken route facts brief; move details to captions/description where more useful |
| Food and boating were described procedurally | Retain real decisions, interaction, the first reaction and sensory sound; avoid routine commentary that adds no new information |
| The ending had repeated questions/CTA and another goodbye | One earned payoff, one comment question, one sign-off |
| Average viewed was 23.13%, but retention data was empty | Treat the earlier-delivery idea as a test; do not claim a particular scene caused drop-off |

## 6. Episode 1 and Episode 2 guidance

### Episode 1 — Bengaluru to Coorg / en-route Golden Temple / first arrival
The saved Episode 1 draft contains the road-trip start, conditional food stops, Namdroling Monastery, check-in, first impression and day-end wrap.

Use the monastery as a genuine story chapter only if the stop actually happened. Its draft has a possible central question about how a site grew from a small beginning into a lasting institution; the voice-over should be built around verified historical details and visible evidence rather than a sequence of generic beauty descriptions. Treat tea, breakfast, resort party and optional stops as conditional—cut them when they did not happen or do not materially contribute.

For the transition to Coorg itself, do not try to carry every theme (coffee, culture, Kaveri, history, all destinations) in the same opening voice-over. Let Episode 1 primarily deliver the road-trip chapter and establish what remains to discover. A short real moment on arrival may be more effective than a broad summary of the whole region.

### Episode 2 — “Inside Coorg” / first impressions and local exploration
The saved draft's first-impression idea starts with misty mountains, coffee plantations and greenery, then moves toward rain, fog and roads between estates. These can be compelling if they are actually present in the footage, but the broad wording risks being a scenic catalogue.

**Working engine to test against the footage:** *What do I notice about Coorg when I slow down enough to experience it rather than simply move between destinations?*

Possible structure:
1. **Hook:** the strongest actual image/sound from the day—mist moving across a hill, rain on leaves, a road through an estate, a first view, or a real unexpected moment. Choose one, not a montage of every motif.
2. **Personal setup:** one natural on-camera line about where you are and what you are trying to experience that day. Avoid the full itinerary.
3. **First discovery:** spend time on one real observation. If a coffee-estate visit happened, ask what the visible scene or guide actually reveals. If the strongest footage is simply walking in mist or rain, use sensory detail and natural sound instead of inventing an explainer.
4. **Reframe:** connect the observation to a second scene only if it adds meaning—such as the difference between the rush of travel and a moment of stillness, or between what you expected and what you actually experienced.
5. **Payoff:** return to the opening image/idea with a specific, honest observation. A grounded line about one moment is stronger than a generic claim that Coorg is peaceful.

Do not lock this structure until the real footage/notes are checked. If Episode 2 is actually driven by a specific offbeat destination, make that the primary engine rather than forcing a slow-nature essay.

### Later Coorg episodes
For every subsequent episode, choose the engine anew from its footage. An adventure or waterfall day may need a personal quest; a historic site may suit question-led investigation; a coffee tour may suit a process question; two contrasting destinations may use contrast-led discovery. Do not repeat the same “Coorg is beautiful and peaceful” thesis in every episode.

## 7. Short field/edit checklist

### Before editing
- [ ] Write the episode's central promise in one sentence.
- [ ] Mark the strongest real moment that can deliver on that promise.
- [ ] Mark first arrival/first evidence and compare it with the video opening.
- [ ] Select 2–3 anchor scenes; classify all other material as support, transition or cut.
- [ ] Mark facts/quotes/translations requiring verification.
- [ ] Decide what the creator OC does that VO should not duplicate.
- [ ] Decide how the ending will answer the opening.

### Before recording VO
- [ ] Does this line add a fact, question, contrast, consequence or personal meaning?
- [ ] Is the line already spoken on camera or visible in the footage?
- [ ] Is the claim supported by a source or by what was actually observed?
- [ ] Is this a genuine personal reaction, not a scripted emotion?
- [ ] Can the viewer understand the sentence on first listen in natural Roman Hinglish?
- [ ] Could natural sound, a pause or an on-screen fact be stronger?

### Before publishing
- [ ] First tangible delivery arrives early enough for this format and story.
- [ ] Every major destination/side quest serves the episode's promise or is cut.
- [ ] The title's main promise is fulfilled.
- [ ] The ending appears once; CTA doesn't restart the video.
- [ ] Compare average view duration/percentage, audience-retention curve if available, and traffic source with comparable uploads.
- [ ] Treat all performance interpretations as hypotheses unless time-aligned data supports them.

## 8. How this relates to AI

The five-act format is still the AI-ready reference. This playbook is a working layer that tells a future script revision how to apply that reference to Coorg. Do **not** wire it into `StorytellingRewriteService` or any OpenAI prompt until the creator reviews the framework and approves the next stage.
